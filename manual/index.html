1. Quick start: the first 10 minutes

Before driving

  1. Carry the Intelligent Key. The vehicle senses it for unlocking, starting, liftgate operation, and memory features.
  2. Adjust the driver seat, steering wheel, mirrors, and head restraint before moving. Store the position with the door memory buttons if this will be a regular driver.
  3. Pair the phone or plug in / connect Apple CarPlay or Android Auto. Set a user profile if multiple drivers will use the car.
  4. Set climate to AUTO and choose a temperature. Use SYNC when you want all zones to follow the driver's setting.
  5. Confirm driver assistance features are enabled: Driver Assistance in the meter display is where Steering Assist, Lane Assist, Blind Spot Assist, Emergency Brake, Speed Limit Sign, and Parking Aids live.
  6. Practice the electronic shifter, parking brake, and Auto Hold once while parked. They are simple, but different enough from older gear levers to deserve a test.
Three immediate habits. Keep your hands on the wheel when using driver assistance; look around the vehicle even when cameras are active; and remember not to park too close to curbs or take tight turns that could scratch the shiny, expensive 20-inch wheels.

Default setup that works well

  • Drive mode: Standard for normal daily driving.
  • Climate: AUTO, A/C on, recirculation off unless temporarily needed, rear climate on only when passengers need it.
  • Driver assistance: leave emergency braking, blind spot, rear cross traffic, and parking aids on. Treat steering assist and cruise control as convenience features, not autonomy.
  • Camera button: use it proactively in parking lots, garages, driveways, and curbside spaces, not just after shifting to Reverse.
  • Auto Hold: useful in traffic and drive-throughs; turn it off when creeping into a garage or car wash where you want very fine brake control.

2. Key, locks, remote start, and liftgate

Intelligent Key basics

The key fob can remain in a pocket or bag for normal use. With the key nearby, use the exterior door handle buttons to lock or unlock, use the push-button ignition, and use the rear liftgate controls. The fob also has physical buttons for lock, unlock, liftgate, remote start, and panic.

For remote start, lock the vehicle first, then use the remote-start button sequence on the fob. After remote start, get in with the key, press the brake, and press the ignition button once to switch the vehicle into normal ON mode before shifting from Park.

Power liftgate

The liftgate can be opened from the fob, the dash switch, the exterior hatch switch, or the button on the open liftgate. The inside liftgate panel includes a close button and a lock/close button. The lock/close button is useful when unloading at home: press it, step away with the key, and the liftgate closes and locks the vehicle.

Useful habit. Before using the power liftgate in a garage, confirm the hatch will clear the garage door, opener arm, bikes, or storage shelves. If a height memory setting is available in the vehicle settings, set it once and leave it there.

3. Starting, shifting, parking, and drive controls

Start and shut down

To start, carry the key, press the brake, and press the ignition button. Let the engine settle briefly before hard acceleration, especially when cold. To shut down, press the P button to select Park, then press the ignition button off.

Electronic shifter

The shifter is compact. The indicator on the shifter and the meter display confirms the current position. Use the side/forward motion for Reverse, Neutral, and Drive/Manual as labeled. Press the P button on top for Park. Do not rely on muscle memory from a traditional mechanical lever until the motion feels natural.

Manual mode and paddles

The D/M position and steering wheel paddles allow manual ratio selection. This is mostly useful for temporary engine braking, holding a gear on hills, or more responsive control. Normal Drive is the right choice for everyday use.

Electronic parking brake

The parking brake switch is beside the shifter. Pull to apply; press while holding the brake pedal to release. The system may also apply or release automatically depending on vehicle state, but the driver should still confirm the PARK/brake status before leaving the vehicle.

Auto Hold

Auto Hold keeps the vehicle stopped after you release the brake pedal, such as at a light or in a drive-through. Press the Auto Hold button to enable it, come to a complete stop, then release the brake. Press the accelerator to move again. Turn it off when inching very slowly into tight spaces, automated car washes, or situations where you want the vehicle to roll gently as you modulate the brake.

Drive Mode

The D-MODE switch changes throttle, transmission, steering, and assistance behavior. Standard is the best default. Sport is more responsive. Eco softens responses for economy. Snow is for slippery roads. Because this FWD Platinum is not the 4x4/off-road configuration, treat the vehicle as a paved-road family SUV, not a trail vehicle.

4. Driver cockpit, steering wheel, and displays

Left side: audio and meter display

The left steering buttons control volume, audio track/station, and the 12.3-inch vehicle information display. Use the scroll/OK control and arrow/back/menu buttons to move between information pages and settings. This is where many driver-assistance settings live, separate from the center touchscreen settings.

Right side: cruise, ProPILOT, voice, and phone

The blue ProPILOT button activates the driver-assistance cruise system. SET- sets the current speed, RES+ resumes or increases set speed, CANCEL cancels without turning the whole system off, and the following-distance button cycles the gap to the vehicle ahead. The phone and voice buttons control calling and voice recognition.

12.3-inch meter display

The meter can show trip economy, speed, navigation/audio prompts, driver assistance status, tire/vehicle information, warnings, and settings. The Driver Assistance menu is especially important because it controls systems that may not have a separate physical button.

Memory seat and door controls

The driver's door includes window switches, mirror controls, lock/unlock, and memory buttons. To store a driving position, adjust seat, mirrors, and steering wheel as desired, press SET, then press memory position 1 or 2. Link the position to the key/user profile if desired so the car recalls the right setup for each regular driver.

5. NissanConnect, phone, apps, and settings

Home screen

The center display uses large tiles for audio, phone, apps, and connections. The left vertical rail provides quick access to home, audio, CarPlay/phone projection, and the app grid. The lower on-screen shortcuts include notifications, settings, and other quick panels.

Important settings locations

Use the gear icon for All Settings. The most useful areas are Connections, Phone, General, Display, Vehicle, Users, and Customize Menus. Some vehicle and driver-assistance settings are in the meter display instead, so check both places when looking for a behavior setting.

Phone pairing, Apple CarPlay, and Android Auto

Pair phones from Connections or Phone settings. Once paired, the system can handle Bluetooth calling/audio and smartphone projection. For the cleanest owner setup, pair the main phone first, confirm contacts and favorites, then create or assign a user profile. If multiple phones are paired, confirm which one is active for calls and media before driving.

Navigation and Navi-link

Use built-in navigation when you want ProPILOT Assist with Navi-link behavior to account for route information. Phone navigation through CarPlay or Android Auto is often easier, but the vehicle's built-in route data is what supports Navi-link behavior. Keep maps and services current when available through NissanConnect.

Voice and notifications

Use the steering wheel voice button for voice commands and phone assistant access. The small microphone/status indicators on the lower display edge help show when voice input or connected services are active. Keep notification volume and chime settings high enough that safety prompts can be heard over music.

6. Climate, heated/cooled comfort, and visibility

Front automatic climate

AUTO is the normal mode. Set the driver's temperature, press SYNC if both front zones should match, and let the system control fan speed and air distribution. A/C can remain on in most weather because it helps dehumidify the cabin. Use recirculation briefly for odors or very hot initial cooldown, but return to outside air for normal driving and defogging.

Defrost and defog

Front defrost prioritizes windshield clearing and may turn on A/C and outside-air intake automatically. Rear defrost also heats the outside mirrors. Do not block the windshield base vents with papers, dash covers, or accessories.

Heated steering wheel

The heated steering wheel button is on the climate panel and can also be found near the driver side control bank. Once on, the system cycles to maintain warmth. It may not heat if the wheel is already warm.

Heated and ventilated front seats

The front seats provide heating and ventilation. Use low or medium for long drives and high for quick warm-up or cooldown. Avoid thick insulating seat covers because they reduce performance and can overheat or interfere with the seat systems.

Rear climate and heated second row

Rear passengers have their own climate panel and heated outboard captain's chairs. From the front panel, REAR ON/OFF enables rear climate, REAR CTRL gives rear passengers control, and REAR LOCK prevents rear changes. The second-row panel includes temperature, fan, airflow mode, AUTO, seat heat, USB-C charging, and a 120V/150W outlet.

Remote start climate

Remote start can precondition the cabin using intelligent climate behavior. In hot weather it can cool the cabin; in cold weather it can warm and defrost. You still need to enter with the key, press the brake, and press the ignition button before shifting from Park.

7. Seats, second row, third row, and cargo

Second-row captain's chairs

This vehicle has second-row captain's chairs rather than a bench. Each chair has its own armrest, seatbelt, head restraint, heat control, and fold/slide access to the third row. Keep the aisle and seat tracks clear so the chairs slide and fold smoothly.

Third row

The third-row seatbacks fold from the cargo area using the release straps/handles on the seatbacks. Raise or lower one side at a time as needed. Confirm each seatback locks into place before carrying passengers, and make sure cargo does not interfere with the seatback latches or seatbelts.

Cargo configurations

With all rows up, use the underfloor bin and cargo mat for smaller items. With one or both third-row sections folded, the cargo floor expands substantially. With the second row folded, the Pathfinder becomes a large, mostly flat cargo hauler. Do not stack loose cargo above the seatbacks unless it is properly secured.

Rear door sunshades

The rear side windows include pull-up sunshades. They are useful for passengers, heat, and glare. Stow them carefully rather than letting them snap back.

8. Safety basics: belts, airbags, sensors, and habits

Seat belts and seating position

Every occupant should sit upright, with the lap belt low across the hips and the shoulder belt across the chest and shoulder. Do not recline excessively, sit sideways, tuck the belt behind the back, or place hard objects between the body and the belt.

Airbag locations to respect

The Pathfinder has front airbags, side-impact airbags, curtain airbags, knee airbags, and other supplemental restraint components depending on position and equipment. In plain terms: keep bodies, bags, pets, accessories, suction mounts, and cargo out of airbag deployment areas. Do not lean on doors or pillars. Do not place feet on the dash. Do not hang heavy items from grab handles or coat hooks near curtain airbags. Do not cover seatbacks or seat cushions with accessories that could interfere with side airbags or occupant sensors.

Front passenger airbag indicator

The front passenger airbag status light is near the mirror/overhead area. It reflects what the occupant classification system is sensing. Heavy objects on the passenger seat can confuse expectations, and water or harsh cleaning methods on the seat can damage sensors. Keep the passenger seat dry, clean, and used as a seat, not as a cargo shelf for heavy items.

Camera and radar limitations

Driver-assistance sensors can be blocked by dirt, ice, heavy rain, fog, road spray, glare, worn lane markings, unusual road geometry, and objects that are too low, too close, or too fast. Cameras and sensors help, but they do not replace looking, steering, braking, and judging space yourself.

Best safety summary. Use the technology as a second set of eyes and a fatigue reducer. Do not use it as permission to stop paying attention.

9. Driver assistance and ProPILOT Assist with Navi-link

Important equipment note. This Pathfinder has ProPILOT Assist with Navi-link. It is not ProPILOT Assist 2.1 and does not provide hands-free driving. Keep hands on the steering wheel and eyes on the road.

ProPILOT Assist: what it does

ProPILOT Assist combines intelligent cruise control with steering assist. When conditions are right, it helps maintain a set speed, keep distance from a vehicle ahead, and help center the vehicle in its lane. With Navi-link and built-in navigation route data, it can use map information to support smoother speed behavior around certain route features.

How to use it

  1. On a suitable road, press the blue ProPILOT button on the right side of the steering wheel.
  2. Press SET- at the speed you want. Use RES+ or SET- to adjust the set speed.
  3. Use the following-distance button to choose the gap to the vehicle ahead.
  4. Use the steering assist button/settings to enable lane-centering support when lane markings and conditions are appropriate.
  5. Cancel with CANCEL, the brake pedal, or by turning the system off.

When not to use steering assist

Avoid relying on steering assist in construction zones, city streets with unclear lanes, sharp curves, bad weather, glare, heavy rain, snow, poor lane markings, complicated merges, or any situation where you would not comfortably let the car make small steering corrections. Keep hands on the wheel; gloves, steering wheel covers, or gripping only the spokes may make hand detection less reliable.

Other assistance features

  • Blind Spot Assist / Blind Spot Warning: warns of vehicles in adjacent lanes and may help discourage a lane change.
  • Lane Assist / Lane Departure systems: warn or assist when drifting from a lane. They depend on visible lane markings.
  • Emergency braking: can warn and may brake for certain forward hazards. It is not a substitute for following distance.
  • Rear Cross Traffic Alert: warns of vehicles approaching from the sides while backing at low speed. It may miss pedestrians, bicycles, animals, angled traffic, or vehicles blocked by neighboring parked cars.
  • Speed Limit Sign: can read or display recognized speed-limit information, but posted signs and driver judgment control.

10. Parking cameras, sonar, and low-speed help

Camera button

The CAMERA button below the screen activates the camera views without needing to shift into Reverse. This is one of the most useful buttons in the vehicle. Use it before entering a tight garage, approaching a curb, lining up in a parking space, or checking the front corners.

View modes

The screen can show a rear or front camera view, a simulated overhead view, side/front views, and guideline overlays. The icons along the bottom cycle through available views. The overhead view is assembled from multiple cameras, so it is helpful for orientation but not a perfect real-time measurement tool.

Moving Object Detection and parking sensors

Moving Object Detection can alert when the camera view sees movement around the vehicle. Parking sonar can warn about nearby obstacles. These systems can miss very low objects, thin posts, curbs, objects outside sensor range, or objects that blend into the surroundings. Always check mirrors and look around.

Curb and wheel habit. The 20-inch wheels look good and are easy to scrape. Approach curbs slowly, leave extra space when parallel parking, and make right turns wide enough that the rear wheel does not climb or kiss the curb.

11. Lights, wipers, moonroof, mirror, and overhead controls

Overhead controls

The overhead panel controls the panoramic moonroof and sunshade, front map/reading lights, interior light behavior, and the SOS emergency button. Do not press SOS except for an actual emergency. The blank area is unused equipment space on this vehicle.

Moonroof and sunshade

The switches operate the glass and shade separately. Use short presses or holds as needed depending on the direction and desired opening. Before closing, make sure nothing is in the opening. Keep the tracks clean and avoid forcing operation if iced or obstructed.

Exterior lights

Leave headlights in AUTO for normal use. The vehicle will manage headlamps based on lighting conditions. Confirm lights manually in heavy rain, fog, or situations where visibility is poor but ambient light may confuse automatic logic.

Wipers and washers

The right stalk controls front and rear wipers/washers. Do not operate washers when the reservoir is empty, and clear snow/ice before using wipers. Rear wiper and rear washer controls are on the same stalk.

Auto-dimming mirror and garage opener

The rearview mirror area includes auto-dimming behavior and, if equipped/configured, programmable garage-door buttons. Program garage/opening devices while parked, following the opener instructions and avoiding door movement near people or objects.

12. Power outlets, charging, storage, and cargo accessories

Front charging and wireless charging

The front console area includes USB ports, a 12V-style power outlet, and a wireless charging pad area. For best wireless charging, remove thick cases or metal/magnetic accessories and center the phone on the pad. If the phone gets hot or charges inconsistently, plug it in instead.

Second-row power

The second row includes USB-C charging ports and a 120V AC outlet rated up to 150W. That outlet is suitable for small electronics, not high-draw appliances. Do not leave accessories drawing power with the engine off for long periods.

Storage areas

Use the center console, under-console storage shelf, door pockets, seatback pockets, glovebox, cupholders, and underfloor cargo bins. Keep hard loose objects out of the cabin when possible; store them in closed bins or the cargo area.

Roof crossbars

Use the installed crossbars for roof cargo, not the roof rails alone. Distribute weight evenly, secure loads with proper straps, and expect wind noise and handling changes with roof cargo. Use a step stool if needed to avoid dragging cargo across the paint or glass.

13. Interior, leather, wheels, and exterior care

Semi-aniline leather

The Chestnut semi-aniline leather is a major part of the Platinum interior. Vacuum grit promptly because small particles abrade leather. Wipe spills immediately with a clean, dry or lightly damp soft cloth. Avoid harsh cleaners, saddle soap, ammonia cleaners, solvents, oils, polishes, and unapproved protectants. Avoid soaking the seats; water or aggressive cleaners can affect seat electronics and occupant sensors.

For routine care, use a mild interior/leather cleaner approved for automotive coated leather, test first in an inconspicuous area, and follow with a clean damp wipe if the product requires it. Do not scrub perforated leather aggressively; use light pressure and let it dry naturally.

Gloss black trim and screens

Gloss black trim scratches easily. Use a clean microfiber cloth and light pressure. Do not dry-wipe dusty surfaces. For screens and meter lenses, use screen-safe microfiber and cleaner; avoid glass/plastic cleaners that could haze or damage coatings.

Wheels, chrome, and exterior

Wash wheels regularly with a gentle automotive wheel cleaner and rinse thoroughly. Avoid strong acidic or alkaline cleaners. If using tire dressing, use a water-based product lightly and wipe away excess so it does not sling onto the paint. Wash and wax the vehicle often enough to protect the finish, especially after road salt, coastal air, mud, or bug buildup.

Wheel protection reminder. Park with extra curb clearance and take turns wide enough to protect the 20-inch wheels. A slow, cautious turn is cheaper than wheel refinishing.

14. Helpful features easy to miss

CAMERA button

Press it before you need it. It is not only for Reverse; it is for curbs, garage walls, parking lines, and tight front-corner checks.

Close-and-lock liftgate

The liftgate lock button closes the hatch and locks the car after unloading, as long as the key is with you.

Auto Hold

Excellent in stop-and-go traffic, but disable it when creeping into precise spaces.

Rear climate lock

Use REAR LOCK when rear passengers keep changing settings or when you want to manage the entire cabin from the front.

User profiles

Profiles help preserve display, phone, audio, and comfort preferences for different drivers.

Built-in nav for Navi-link

Phone maps may be preferred day to day, but built-in route guidance is what supports Navi-link behavior.

Underfloor cargo bins

Good for emergency items, straps, umbrellas, small bags, and things you do not want sliding around.

Second-row outlet

The 120V outlet is convenient for laptops and small electronics, limited to 150W.
